## At a Glance

* **The Offender:** Amazon Europe Core S.à r.l.
* **The Authority:** Commission Nationale pour la Protection des Données (CNPD), Luxembourg
* **The Penalty:** €746,000,000 (€746 Million) + order to revise targeting practices
* **The Core Issue:** Building automated behavioral profiles and targeting personalized ads without obtaining valid, freely given consent.

---

## 1. The TL;DR in Plain English

Every time you browse Amazon, click products, read reviews, or view categories, Amazon's algorithms take note. In 2018, French digital rights group *La Quadrature du Net* filed a collective complaint signed by over 10,000 citizens. 

They alleged that Amazon collected massive troves of personal browsing and purchase behavior to build silent advertising profiles—and that Amazon failed to ask for clear, opt-in consent before doing so. Luxembourg's privacy authority agreed, ruling that "bundled consent" hidden in terms and conditions is completely illegal under the GDPR.

---

## 2. The Story: What Happened?

Amazon argued that profiling users to recommend products was a fundamental part of the e-commerce service users signed up for (relying on "contractual necessity" under Article 6(1)(b)) or that it had a "legitimate interest" in serving relevant ads (Article 6(1)(f)).

The CNPD completely rejected both arguments:
* **Buying a book or toaster does not require behavioral profiling:** An online store can fulfill an order and process shipping without building a deep psychological profile of the customer's interests.
* **Consent cannot be forced:** Amazon effectively gave consumers an all-or-nothing ultimatum: agree to our privacy policy and profiling, or don't use Amazon. Under GDPR, consent obtained under duress or bundled into non-negotiable terms is legally void.

---

## 3. What Caught Them?

The investigation revealed that Amazon maintained an automated ad-targeting ecosystem that linked customer purchase history, search queries, external device identifiers, and third-party partner data. 

Because Amazon had made Luxembourg its official European headquarters, the Luxembourg CNPD was appointed Lead Supervisory Authority. Following a 3-year forensic investigation, the CNPD concluded that millions of European shoppers had their personal data fed into an ad-targeting engine without ever being given a fair, transparent choice.

---

## 4. The Legal Violation: Why the Fine Happened

The decision targeted core provisions of GDPR Chapter II:

### Article 5(1)(a) (Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency)
Data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject. Secret profiling violates fairness because the user cannot reasonably anticipate how their clicks are monetized.

### Article 6(1)(a) & Article 7 (Conditions for Consent)
Where processing is based on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject has consented. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous. Amazon failed to meet any of these standards.

---

## 5. What This Means for Privacy Teams & CIPP/E Students

1. **AdTech Cannot Hide Behind "Contractual Necessity":** Serving targeted behavioral ads is almost never strictly necessary to perform a contract for goods or services.
2. **Pre-Ticked Boxes & Bundled Terms are Dead:** Any consent mechanism that bundles marketing profiling with core account creation is invalid.
3. **Fines Scale with Global Turnover:** The €746M fine demonstrated that DPAs are willing to invoke Article 83(5)'s maximum 4% of worldwide annual turnover against Big Tech.
