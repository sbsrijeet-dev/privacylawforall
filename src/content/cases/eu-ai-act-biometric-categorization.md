## At a Glance

* **The Law:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (The EU AI Act)
* **The Enforcement Body:** European AI Office (DG CONNECT) & National Market Surveillance Authorities
* **Maximum Statutory Fine:** €35,000,000 or **7% of global annual turnover** (whichever is higher)
* **The Core Target:** The complete ban on AI practices that violate fundamental human dignity, manipulate human behavior, or conduct untargeted biometric facial scraping.

---

## 1. The TL;DR in Plain English

While GDPR protects personal data, the EU AI Act regulates the *algorithms and models* themselves. It establishes a risk-based pyramid with four levels: Unacceptable Risk, High Risk, Specific Transparency Risk, and Minimal Risk.

If an AI system falls into the **Unacceptable Risk** category, putting it on the European market is completely illegal. Violating these prohibitions carries penalties that make GDPR fines look modest: **up to €35 Million or 7% of your company's worldwide annual revenue**.

---

## 2. The Banned List: What AI Practices Are Strictly Prohibited (Article 5)

Article 5 draws clear legal red lines across eight specific technologies:

1. **Subliminal or Deceptive Manipulation:** AI that uses techniques beyond a person's consciousness to distort behavior and cause physical or psychological harm.
2. **Exploiting Vulnerabilities:** AI targeting age, disability, or specific socio-economic conditions to manipulate decisions to someone's detriment.
3. **Social Scoring:** Government or corporate systems that score individuals based on social behavior or personality traits, leading to unjustified detrimental treatment.
4. **Predictive Policing Based Solely on Profiling:** Using algorithms to assess the likelihood of a person committing a crime based purely on traits or personality profiling without verifiable criminal acts.
5. **Untargeted Facial Scraping (The Clearview AI Clause):** Creating or expanding facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage.
6. **Emotion Recognition in Workplaces & Schools:** Systems designed to infer emotions of employees or students, except for strictly medical or safety reasons.
7. **Biometric Categorization of Sensitive Attributes:** Using biometric data to deduce race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious beliefs, or sexual orientation.

## 3. High-Risk AI Systems (Article 6 & Annex III)

Systems that are not banned but pose significant risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights are classified as **High-Risk AI**. Examples include:
* Critical infrastructure (traffic management, water/power supply).
* Education and vocational training (automated grading, admissions screening).
* Employment and HR (AI resume screeners, promotion evaluation).
* Essential private and public services (credit scoring, loan approvals, welfare eligibility).
* Law enforcement and immigration control.

### Mandatory Requirements for High-Risk AI:
* **Risk Management System (Article 9):** Continuous risk evaluation throughout the AI lifecycle.
* **High-Quality Training Datasets (Article 10):** Training data must be relevant, representative, and checked for bias and errors.
* **Human Oversight (Article 14):** Systems must be designed so natural persons can oversee, intervene, or override AI decisions ("human-in-the-loop").
* **Cybersecurity & Robustness (Article 15):** Resilience against adversarial prompt attacks, data poisoning, and model evasion.

## 4. The Penalties: Why Tech Executives Are Paying Attention

The EU AI Act's fining structure eclipses GDPR:

| Violation Category | Penalty Ceiling |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prohibited AI Practices (Article 5)** | **€35 Million or 7% of global turnover** |
| **High-Risk AI Non-Compliance** | **€15 Million or 3% of global turnover** |
| **Supplying Misleading Info to Regulators** | **€7.5 Million or 1.5% of global turnover** |

## 5. What This Means for Privacy Teams & AIGP Students

1. **Facial Recognition Scraping is Dead in the EU:** Scraping public social media or web photos to build facial recognition vectors violates both GDPR Article 9 and AI Act Article 5.
2. **HR Tech is Front and Center:** Resume screeners and interview analysis tools are High-Risk AI and must undergo conformity assessments and register in the EU public database.
3. **Dual Enforcement:** A company deploying an unlawful AI recruitment tool can face simultaneous investigations: one from the Data Protection Authority under GDPR, and another from the Market Surveillance Authority under the EU AI Act.
